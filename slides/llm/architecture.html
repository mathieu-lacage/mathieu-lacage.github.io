Attention

Multi-Head Attention

A layer runs H heads side by side, here H = 4. Input x: n × d, with d = H · dh

One product computes Q, K and V: x · [WQ | WK | WV], each W is d × d

Head 1 only uses its column slice of each W: d × dh, as on the previous slide

Regroup the slices by head: head h owns Qh, Kh, Vh, n × dh; the same x feeds them all

Each head computes its own attention Ah = softmax(Qh KhT / √dh + M)

Zh = Ah · Vh, n × dh: Ah to the left, Vh above, as on the previous slide

Side by side, the Zh form Z, n × d

WO is d × d, cut into H row slices of dh rows

Head 1 adds Z1 · WO(1), n × d, to O

Head 2 adds Z2 · WO(2)

Head 3 adds Z3 · WO(3)

Head 4 adds Z4 · WO(4): O = Σh Zh · WO(h) = Z · WO, n × d


def multihead(X, M, Wq, Wk, Wv, Wo, H=4):
    """H heads side by side; d = H * dh"""
    n, d = X.shape
    Q, K, V = X @ Wq, X @ Wk, X @ Wv
    Qh = np.split(Q, H, axis=1)
    Kh = np.split(K, H, axis=1)
    Vh = np.split(V, H, axis=1)
    # head h owns Qh[h], Kh[h], Vh[h]: n x dh each
    A = [softmax(Qh[h] @ Kh[h].T / np.sqrt(d // H) + M) for h in range(H)]
    Zh = [A[h] @ Vh[h] for h in range(H)]
    Z = np.hstack(Zh)
    Wo_h = np.split(Wo, H, axis=0)
    O = np.zeros((n, d))
    for h in range(H):
        O += Zh[h] @ Wo_h[h]
    return O

Grouped-Query Attention

Same layer, H = 4 query heads, but only nkv = 2 key / value heads

WQ is still d × d, but WK and WV are only d × nkv dh: fewer weights, smaller K and V

Head 1 uses its slice of WQ, and slice 1 of WK and WV

Regroup by key / value head: heads 1 and 2 share K1, V1; heads 3 and 4 share K2, V2

Each query head still has its own attention: A1 = softmax(Q1 K1T …), A2 = softmax(Q2 K1T …): same keys, different queries

Z1 = A1 · V1 and Z2 = A2 · V1: the same values, mixed differently

From here on, nothing changes: the Zh form Z, n × d

WO is d × d, cut into H row slices

Head 1 adds Z1 · WO(1) to O

Head 2 adds Z2 · WO(2)

Head 3 adds Z3 · WO(3)

Head 4 adds Z4 · WO(4): O = Z · WO, n × d, as in multi-head attention

Grouped-query attention (GQA): n_q query heads share n_kv key / value heads, n_q / n_kv query heads per group. n_kv = n_q is multi-head attention, n_kv = 1 multi-query attention (MQA). Qwen3-1.7B: 16 query heads, 8 KV heads, the same 2 per group as here; Llama-2-7B is plain MHA. Why: K and V are what decoding keeps for every past token (the KV cache, in « Inference »); halving the KV heads halves the cache, at a small cost in quality.

Multi-Query Attention

Same layer, H = 4 query heads, but a single key / value head: nkv = 1

WQ is still d × d, but WK and WV are only d × dh: one slice each

Head 1 uses its slice of WQ, and the only slice of WK and WV

Regroup by key / value head: there is only one, so all 4 query heads share K and V

Each query head still has its own attention: Ah = softmax(Qh KT …): one set of keys, 4 different queries

Zh = Ah · V: the same values, mixed 4 different ways

From here on, nothing changes: the Zh form Z, n × d

WO is d × d, cut into H row slices

Head 1 adds Z1 · WO(1) to O

Head 2 adds Z2 · WO(2)

Head 3 adds Z3 · WO(3)

Head 4 adds Z4 · WO(4): O = Z · WO, n × d, as in multi-head attention

Multi-query attention (MQA, Shazeer 2019, « One Write-Head is All You Need »): GQA with n_kv = 1. The KV cache shrinks by H (here 4, often 32 or more in real models), which is what decode is bound by. The cost is quality and training stability, hence GQA as the middle ground (Ainslie et al. 2023 convert MHA checkpoints to GQA). Used by PaLM, Falcon-7B, StarCoder.

Mixture of Experts

The FFN of the previous slides: every token goes through the same Up and Down

Create 8 independent FFNs, each with its own weights: the experts

A router: x · Wrouter, then softmax: 8 scores per token

« Paris » keeps its top 2 scores: expert 3 (0.62) and expert 6 (0.38)

« Paris » goes through FFN 3 and FFN 6 only: the other 6 are not computed

« is »: its top 2 are experts 1 and 6, then the same multiply and sum

« the »: experts 1 and 4

« capital »: experts 6 and 8. Each token does the work of 2 FFNs, out of 8 stored

An expert is just a copy of the FFN of the previous slides (Up → wide vector → Down), with its own weights. The router picks 2 of the 8 per token; the other 6 are never computed for that token. The routing here is a cartoon. Qwen3-30B-A3B (local config.json): d = 2 048, 128 experts of d_ff = 768, 8 per token, 48 layers.

The router

« Paris »: its vector x, d numbers, as it enters the FFN layer

The router is one matrix, Wrouter: d rows, one column per expert

Score of expert 1: x · column 1, a dot product

Score of expert 2: x · column 2

And so on: x Wrouter gives 8 scores, one per expert

Softmax: the 8 scores become probabilities that sum to 1

Position

Normalization

Pre-norm, post-norm

The same block twice: the only difference is where the norm sits

Pre-norm: the norm sits inside the branch, just before attention

The residual stream goes straight through: attention is added back, never normalized

Same for the FFN: a normalized copy goes in, its output is added back

Post-norm: the norm sits on the stream, after the add: LayerNorm(x + Attn(x))

The normalized stream feeds the FFN, its output is added, normalized again — every layer

Pre-norm leaves the skips untouched: gradients flow, no warm-up — every modern model

The price: the stream is never normalized, so one last norm before the unembedding

Pre-norm (2019–): the norms live inside the branches, the residual stream is never touched. GPT-2 moved the norm there; Llama, Qwen3, everything modern is pre-norm. Post-norm: the norm sits on the stream after each add — the original Transformer (Vaswani 2017) and BERT. Why pre-norm won: Xiong et al. 2020, « On Layer Normalization in the Transformer Architecture » — in post-norm, the gradient of every layer passes through a norm, which shrinks it and requires a learning-rate warm-up; pre-norm trains without one. Nguyen & Salazar 2019 say it first. The price of pre-norm: the stream comes out unnormalized, so one final norm is needed before the unembedding (Qwen3 config: model.norm). Post-norm needs none. Optional 4th slide if asked: sandwich norm (Gemma 2/3, OLMo 2) — pre-norm plus an extra norm after the sub-layer, inside the branch.

LayerNorm → RMSNorm

« Paris » enters the norm: its vector x, d = 8 numbers. Two norms, same job

LayerNorm: subtract the mean μ = 0.5 — the vector is centered

Divide by the standard deviation σ = 1.5: unit variance, values within ±1.67

Scale by γ, one learned number per coordinate, and add β

RMSNorm drops the mean and β: divide by the root mean square, RMS = √2.5 ≈ 1.58

y = γ ⊙ x / RMS — the same job, fewer ops: no μ, no β

Qwen3-1.7B: RMSNorm, ε = 1e-6 — two per layer + a final one, γ of d = 2 048

LayerNorm: Ba, Kiros, Hinton 2016. RMSNorm: Zhang & Sennrich 2019 — drop mean-centering and the bias, keep only the rescaling. The worked x = [1, 2, −1, 0, 3, −2, 1, 0]: mean 0.5, mean of squares 2.5, so σ = √(2.5 − 0.25) = 1.5 and RMS = √2.5 ≈ 1.58. ε guards the division (Qwen3: rms_norm_eps = 1e-6, negligible at d = 2048). In practice ε is inside the √ with the variance or the mean of squares. Qwen3-1.7B: two RMSNorms per layer (input_layernorm, post_attention_layernorm) plus the final model.norm — each a single γ of d = 2 048, ≈ 2 048 weights per norm. Cost: these norms are element-wise work, counted in the cost tables' 10 n d per layer (« softmax, SiLU, norms »): ≈ 5 ops per number per norm, × 2 norms. LayerNorm would add the mean subtraction and the β — RMSNorm is also a small win there.

QK-norm

Back in the attention layer: one head's q, k, v — slices of x · WQ, x · WK, x · WV

Qwen3 passes q and k through an RMSNorm before using them; v goes untouched

One norm per head: dh numbers, its own γ of dh — 16 query heads, 8 KV heads

Then RoPE (the Position section), then the usual A = softmax(q kT / √dh + M)

Why: q and k meet in a dot product — the norm keeps their logits bounded

Qwen2: a learned bias on q, k, v. Qwen3: biases dropped, these two norms instead

Qwen3-1.7B: q_norm and k_norm, an RMSNorm over head_dim = 128 applied to each head's q and k right after the projection, before RoPE (tools/qkv_map.py: `at.q_norm(at.q_proj(x).view(T, nH, dh))`). Each owns a γ of d_h = 128; the 16 query heads' q_norms and the 8 KV heads' k_norms are separate. Qwen2 had a bias on Q, K, V instead (Qwen2 config: qkv bias) — also a stability hack. Qwen3 dropped the biases and added q_norm/k_norm (Qwen2 had no q/k norm). Precedent for QK-norm at scale: ViT-22B (Dehghani et al. 2023). Effect: q and k enter the logits through their dot product; with unnormalized q, k a few heads grow large norms and their logits blow up. The visible symptom is the attention sink (« is a BOS token a sink? » in inference.html, data/trace-Qwen3-1.7B-sink.json).

Sandwich norm

Pre-norm recap: x + Attn(Norm(x)) — the stream itself is never normalized

Sandwich norm: a second norm inside the branch — x + Norm(Attn(Norm(x)))

Why: the stream grows without bound — measured, × 2 500 across Qwen3's 28 layers

Every add is now a normalized vector: no sub-layer can blow up the stream

Qwen3's fix is one-off at the end (3296 → 110); the sandwich norm bounds every layer

Gemma 2 / 3 and OLMo 2 train this way; Qwen3 keeps plain pre-norm + QK-norm

Sandwich norm: an extra norm on the branch output, before the add — pre-norm and post-norm in the same sub-layer. Gemma 2/3 (RMSNorm before AND after each sub-layer) and OLMo 2 (their « Post-Norm » placement: x + Norm(attn(norm(x)))). Why it might be needed: in plain pre-norm, nothing ever normalizes the stream — our own Qwen3-1.7B trace (data/Qwen3-1.7B.json, residual_norm) shows ‖x « Paris »‖ growing 1.3 → 3296 over the 28 layers (× 2 500), then the final norm pulling it to 110. Each sub-layer's output is added to the stream with whatever norm it happens to have; a few coordinates grow huge (the massive activations tied to attention sinks). The sandwich norm makes every contribution a normalized vector: growth per layer is bounded by construction, no single branch can push the stream around, and late layers see sane inputs. OLMo 2 reports it trains more stably; Gemma 2 pairs it with logit soft-capping for stability at scale. Cost: one extra element-wise norm per sub-layer — ≈ 5 n d ops and a γ of d — noise next to the 2 n d² matmuls. The final norm still stays (Gemma and OLMo 2 keep one). Qwen3 does NOT use a sandwich norm: its stability tools are QK-norm (previous slide); the stream is left free to grow and only rescaled once, by model.norm, before the unembedding. Trade-off to discuss: sandwich norm bounds growth layer by layer; the final norm is free but the unnormalized stream is what attention and the FFN consume — which is exactly why each sub-layer starts with an input norm.
